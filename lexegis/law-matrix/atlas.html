Digital consumer law

Every classification as a vector, so proximity is a measurement.

Thirty-two bodies of digital consumer law, each scored against sixteen normative primitives — what the law actually demands, rather than what it is called. With the field as a matrix, questions that were matters of opinion become computations: which regimes argue alike, what a jurisdiction systematically over-weights, and where one set of terms cannot be lawful everywhere.

Method

How the numbers were made

Computation

The classification vectors and the coverage tensor are scored per row against the instruments cited in the dossier. Wolfram Language computes the cosine geometry, the agglomerative clustering, the singular-value embedding, the primitive entropies and correlations, and the jurisdiction statistics. A second implementation re-derives every shared quantity and the two are compared before anything is published, so a transcription slip fails loudly instead of shipping.

One statistic was corrected during the build: cosine similarity over raw exposure sums put every pair of forums between 0.997 and 1.000, because each forum sums the same thirty-two rows and so points the same way. Emphasis — exposure per unit of coverage, standardised per primitive — is what actually discriminates, and it is what Plate III shows.

What this is not

Scores are an analyst scaffold, not a source of law. Each dossier carries a verify note naming what to re-check — a phase-in date, a designation, a national transposition — because those are the details that decide cases, and they move faster than any matrix.

Reading the scales

A research instrument for comparative analysis. Not legal advice; no lawyer-client relationship. Instruments named in the dossiers are the sources to read — this page is an index to them, not a substitute for them.
